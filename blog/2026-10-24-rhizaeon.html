Methods & Reticulate Evolution

Clean breaks: tracking recombination across whole genomes in seconds

24 Oct 2026, by Sergei · 4 min read · 60-sec slide deck · Preprint: bioRxiv 2026.10.19.760237
Key Takeaways

Recombination detectors have long forced an uncomfortable trade-off: phylogenetic tree-partitioning models bog down on whole genomes, whereas fast sequence-triplet scanners count a single ancestral crossover dozens of times and mistake fast-evolving branches for hybrids. By tracking sequences as continuous trajectories across a metric embedding in linear $\mathcal{O}(NKL)$ time, RhizAeon holds false positives near 1% across a million simulated genomes while reconstructing sarbecovirus Spike origins, 2.22 Mb bacterial chromosomes, and primate MHC micro-conversions in seconds inside a web browser.

Twenty years ago, when David Posada, Simon Frost, and I built GARD, we wanted a reliable way to stop homologous recombination from breaking phylogenetic trees and manufacturing false positives in $dN/dS$ selection scans (Murrell et al., 2012; Murrell et al., 2015). GARD works well on single genes, but fitting separate maximum-likelihood trees across candidate breakpoint partitions scales poorly with both genome length and taxon count. When Spyros Lytras, Darren Martin, David Robertson, and I analyzed 78 complete sarbecovirus genomes ($30{,}956\text{ nt}$) to map the recombinant origins of SARS-CoV-2 (Lytras et al., 2022), we had to downsample the alignment to 18 genomes just to finish a GARD run in 4.5 days of cluster compute.

In a new preprint with Danielle Callan, Steven Weaver, Hannah Verdonk, Jordan Zehr, Spyros Lytras, Anton Nekrutenko, Taylor Paisie, and Darren Martin (open-source Rust/Python code, browser app, and Zenodo archive), we introduce RhizAeon—a recombination engine that replaces combinatorial tree search and triplet enumeration with continuous trajectory tracking in linear $\mathcal{O}(NKL)$ time.

Why not just run a fast sequence-triplet scanner like 3SEQ or RDP5? Evaluating $\binom{N}{3}$ triplets for runs of informative sites is fast on small virus alignments, but testing triplets in isolation creates four blind spots on modern datasets (Fig. 1):

  • Ancestral clade overcounting: If a single recombination event occurred in the ancestor of a 10-sequence clade, testing every descendant against every pair of donor cousins triggers $\mathcal{O}(C \cdot D^2)$ redundant hits. On simulated 10-child recombinant clades, 3SEQ reports an average of $14.37 \pm 7.27$ separate events for a single historical crossover (Fig. 1B).
  • Rate bursts mistaken for recombination: When one viral or bacterial lineage speeds up—or undergoes an APOBEC hypermutation burst—its genetic distance to outgroup taxa shifts abruptly without any DNA exchange.
  • Extinct ("ghost") donors and multi-parent mosaics: Standard triplet tests assume both parental lineages sit inside your sample and that every recombinant has exactly two parents. When the donor lineage goes extinct or remains unsampled (Fig. 1C), or when serial transfers splice three or more donors into the same chromosome (Fig. 1D), two-parent triplet tests break down.

How do we get the whole-alignment discipline of a phylogenetic model at linear $\mathcal{O}(NKL)$ speed? Instead of rebuilding trees in sliding windows or checking $\binom{N}{3}$ triplets, RhizAeon embeds $K$ landmark reference sequences into low-dimensional metric coordinates $\mathbf{x}_k \in \mathbb{R}^d$ via classical multidimensional scaling (Gower, 1966) and tracks each sequence $i$ along the alignment $u$ as a smooth attention weight vector $\mathbf{w}_i(u)$ over those references—plus one extra coordinate at the origin, an unpolarized root sink ($w_{i,0}$):

$$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}_i(u)) = \frac{1}{2}\left\lVert \mathbf{r}_i(u) - \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_{i,k}(u)\,\mathbf{x}_k - \beta_i(u)\,\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{ghost}} \right\rVert_2^2 + \gamma \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_{i,k}(u)\,\widetilde{D}_{ik}(u) - \tau \sum_{k=0}^{K} w_{i,k}(u)\ln w_{i,k}(u)$$

When a lineage simply evolves faster on its own branch, its distances to all references rise together and flow harmlessly into the root sink $w_{i,0}$. Across 1,200 coalescent rate-variation nulls and 1,000,000 simulated whole genomes spanning six orders of magnitude of mutation and recombination rates, RhizAeon holds its false-positive rate at 0.58% and 1.01%—well below the 5% nominal ceiling (Fig. 2). When a true crossover occurs, the trajectory $\mathbf{w}_i(u)$ snaps from one donor coordinate to another (or activates the orthogonal ghost vector $\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{ghost}}$ when a tract diverges from all sampled references). A Brownian-bridge filter and 5-state Viterbi decoder lock in the breakpoints, iteratively scrub the mosaic tract out of the alignment to uncover older nested layers, and merge co-recombinant clades into a single ancestral event.

What does linear-time metric tracking unlock on real viral, bacterial, and vertebrate datasets? Three empirical benchmarks tell the story:

  • 78–83 whole sarbecovirus genomes in 2.6 seconds (vs. 4.5 days in GARD): Running RhizAeon directly on the full 78-genome sarbecovirus alignment from Lytras et al. (2022) ($30{,}956\text{ nt}$) plus 5 Laotian BANAL bat coronaviruses (Temmam et al., 2022) finishes in 2.6 seconds on a laptop (Fig. 3). Without downsampling, it recovers all 21 whole-genome breakpoints inferred by 4.5 days of GARD with a median offset of 99.0 nt—matching the critical Spike NTD ($21{,}825\text{ bp}$) and S1/S2 ($23{,}617\text{ bp}$) junctions within 9 to 12 nucleotides—and directly isolates the BANAL-20-52 / RaTG13 receptor-binding domain (RBD) cassette exchange ($p < 10^{-16}$).
  • A 2.22-megabase Streptococcus pneumoniae chromosome in 36.1 seconds: Across 240 multidrug-resistant S. pneumoniae PMEN1 whole-chromosome alignments ($2{,}221{,}315\text{ bp}$, Croucher et al., 2011), RhizAeon maps 392 horizontal gene transfer tracts in 36.1 seconds (~35× faster than Gubbins), pinpointing the exact resistance cassettes at penicillin-binding proteins pbp2x, pbp1a, and pbp2b, dihydrofolate reductase folA/folP, and the serotype 23F $\to$ 19A capsule switch locus (cps).
  • 86–150 bp micro-conversions across 379 primate MHC Class I alleles in 1.1 seconds: In hominoid HLA-A, HLA-B, and HLA-C alleles (Parham et al., 1995), gene conversion swaps tiny 50–150 bp Exon 2/3 cassettes that remodel peptide-binding pockets without disturbing flanking introns. RhizAeon's dyadic matched filter detects 103 conversion tracts in 1.1 seconds—including the classic inter-locus HLA-B*46:01 hybrid spliced between HLA-B*15:01 and HLA-C*01:02 across nucleotides 462–607 ($p = 2.8 \times 10^{-8}$).

Next up, we are wiring RhizAeon's linear-time partition maps directly into HyPhy 3 and HyphAeon so that every selection and molecular-clock scan can automatically partition mosaic chromosomes in seconds before testing $dN/dS$.
