Rethinking recombination from first principles with AI-accelerated algorithm design

Instead of porting discrete tree searches or triplet loops to faster hardware, we formulated recombination as continuous motion across a metric reference map in linear $\mathcal{O}(NKL)$ time:

Slide 2 of 6 · How the RhizAeon Stack Works

Attention maps, force dipoles, and dyadic polishing in four steps

1. Map + Center Drain
Landmarks & Root Sink ($w_{i,0}$)

Places $K$ reference genomes on a map with a neutral drain at the center. Fast-mutating branches flow into the center drain instead of triggering false alarms.

2. Attention Maps
Where Each Genome Points ($\mathbf{w}_i(u)$)

At each site $u$, every sequence distributes attention weights over the landmarks. Unsampled ("ghost") donors lift the sequence along an orthogonal ghost axis $\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{ghost}}$.

3. Force Dipoles
Push From Old Parent, Pull to New

When a sequence jumps from Parent A to Parent B, Parent A's weight drops and Parent B's weight rises equally—creating a sharp force dipole ($\Delta \mathbf{w}_i$) tested against a Brownian bridge.

4. Dyadic Polishing
Wavelets, Viterbi & Scrubbing

Wavelet filters with doubling widths ($2, 4, 8, 16\dots$ sites) catch both 80 bp and 30 kb swaps. Viterbi decoding and likelihood polishing lock in exact edges, scrub tracts, and merge clades.

Slide 3 of 6 · Four Failure Modes Fixed

Rate bursts, ancestral clades, ghost donors, and three-parent mosaics

(A) The neutral center drain $w_{i,0}$ keeps false alarms at $\alpha = 0.05$ under strong branch rate bursts. (B) On 10-child recombinant clades, clade merging collapses shared tracts into $1.01 \pm 0.08$ ancestral events (98.8% exact) vs. $14.37$ calls in 3SEQ. (C–D) Orthogonal ghost projection finds extinct donors (89.2% F1) and three-parent mosaics (90.0% full parent recovery).
