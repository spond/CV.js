Bite 1 of 6 • The Local Reproducibility Crisis

Hand One Dataset to 30 Local Coding Agents—Get 18 Incompatible Pipelines

Autonomous coding agents can design and execute a complete multi-step study from a single prompt. But what happens when they improvise in isolated local terminals?

We handed a published bacterial RNA-seq study of Bacillus cereus enterotoxin regulation (Vyklická et al., 2025) to 30 independent frontier agents—10 Claude Opus, 10 OpenAI Codex, and 10 Google Gemini—and asked each for a replication plan.

Exposed to two routine ambiguities (a suppressed RefSeq assembly after taxonomic reclassification to B. pacificus, and whether DESeq2 pooled all 19 libraries or only 5 focal samples), 29 of the 30 agents diverged from the paper, splintering into 18 distinct pipelines.

Silent biological distortion: When executed locally, aligning to B. pacificus completely erased an actively transcribed toxin gene (cytK2, ~800 read pairs/library on ATCC 10987) and left behind 26 unversioned glue scripts (>1,200 lines of code).
Bite 2 of 6 • Public Infrastructure

Moving Agentic Science from Private Black Boxes onto the Galactic Plane

Today's vision of "agentic science" is largely being built behind closed doors—running on laptops or paid commercial clouds where intermediate files vanish when the session ends.

We took the opposite path. We connected CLI coding agents—and Orbit, our open-source desktop workbench built on Pi.dev—directly into UseGalaxy.org and free national ACCESS-CI supercomputers (Stampede3, Bridges-2, Expanse, Anvil, Jetstream2) via curated domain skills and a typed Galaxy Model Context Protocol (MCP) server.

Any researcher or student can drive multi-core CPUs, terabyte-RAM nodes, and NVIDIA GPUs at zero cost under one rule: the agent cannot hide its tracks.

Permanent provenance by design: Instead of throwaway local scripts, bespoke code is packaged into containerized User-Defined Tools (UDTs), and every input, parameter, and output is locked into an inspectable Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) and living research notebook.
Bite 3 of 6 • Forensic Audit I

When an Agent Benchmark Silently Runs a Demo File: The 82% Missing-Data Trap

What if a landmark Nature paper about reproducible agentic science itself fell prey to an unverified agentic shortcut—and our public provenance harness caught it?

In Paper2Agent (Miao et al., 2026), an agent wrapped the spatial transcriptomics tool TISSUE and cited a 5,613-cell mouse somatosensory cortex atlas (Dataset 15). Yet when we audited the result on Galaxy, we discovered that the benchmark had actually been run on a 1,000-cell unit-test demo file (tests/data/scRNA_count.txt) bundled inside the GitHub repo—silently discarding 82% of the reference cells!

On the 1,000-cell demo subset, predicted Acta2 expression was nearly uncorrelated with ground truth ($r = 0.0861$). Running TISSUE on UseGalaxy.org reproduced their 1,000-cell tutorial output to floating-point precision ($r = 1.0000$) and scaled to all 5,613 cells—tripling prediction accuracy to $r = 0.2553$.

Why provenance matters: Without an auditable trail of input file digests and dimensions, an agent substituting a 1,000-cell unit test for a 5,613-cell atlas looks like a successful run.
Bite 4 of 6 • Forensic Audit II

Exposing Unstated Pooling, Unmerged Replicates, and Annotation Traps

Across 48 biological analyses, keeping every intermediate table in the open turned our agents into automated forensic auditors of peer-reviewed papers:

• Aspergillus fumigatus (30-library azole time-course): Pairwise DESeq2 missed 39% of published DEGs until our agent spotted identical baseMean values across all 5 timepoints—proving the authors had fitted one pooled 30-sample model (25 residual d.f.). Switching to the pooled design matched published counts within 0.3–1.3% ($r \ge 0.9996$).
• Anopheles gambiae (infection ATAC-seq): Reproduced 90.7–93.2% of chromatin peaks while proving headline region counts came from unmerged replicate rows.
• Aedes aegypti (midgut scRNA-seq, 6,707 cells): Fixed a non-model AAEL mitochondrial prefix that caused standard Scanpy QC to report 0% mitochondrial reads.

Bite 5 of 6 • Discovery at Scale I

Unifying 2,592 Malaria Surface Antigens Across Three Continents on Public GPUs

Moving from auditing past studies to new discovery, we directed Orbit to resolve the hypervariable VIR surface protein repertoire across three Plasmodium vivax genomes from Indonesia, Thailand, and Peru (2,592 proteins).

Because VIR proteins diverge into the sequence twilight zone ($\le 30\%$ amino acid identity), sequence clustering fractures them into 146–203 fragmented groups, obscuring whether parasites on different continents use distinct immune-evasion scaffolds.

By registering 10 unprivileged UDTs on UseGalaxy.org to orchestrate ProstT5 (3Di protein language model), Foldseek, FoldMason, and IQ-TREE 3 on national ACCESS-CI GPUs, Orbit collapsed the entire repertoire into 73–76 structural classes—showing that over 97% of all VIR proteins belong to 21 conserved ancestral folds shared across all three continents ($p = 0.55$).

Zero local GPU required: Any researcher can orchestrate heavyweight protein language models and structural phylogenetics on national GPU allocations directly through Galaxy.
Bite 6 of 6 • Discovery at Scale II

Dating 42 Pathogen Epidemics in Seconds Instead of Days of MCMC

Finally, we wrapped ChronAeon as Galaxy User-Defined Tools and tasked Orbit with dating 42 published viral and bacterial BEAST benchmark alignments (41 unique cohorts across 37 studies, including Ebola, SARS-CoV-2, Zika, Dengue, Mpox, H1N1, Rabies, and Yersinia pestis).

Traditional Bayesian molecular clock inference (`BEAST`) requires hours to days of Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling per dataset. On UseGalaxy.org, Orbit executed all 42 cohorts in seconds per alignment, matching reference Bayesian emergence ($t_{\text{MRCA}}$) intervals across 61.5–64.1% of scored outbreaks with a median error of just 0.40 years for post-1980 epidemics.

The takeaway: Anchoring autonomous agents to open-source tools and free public supercomputing turns a looming reproducibility crisis into a permanent, verifiable engine for discovery.
