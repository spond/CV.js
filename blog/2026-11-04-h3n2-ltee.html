Tight Locks, Narrow Side Doors: What 1,200 Generations of Lab-Evolved H3N2 Flu Reveal About Protein Constraint

04 Nov 2026, by Sergei · 4 min read · Cohort: 12 Replicate H3N2 Populations · 100 Passages (1,200 Generations, 4,477 Codons)
What Does This All Mean?

When de Rooij et al. (2026) evolved human seasonal influenza A/H3N2 across 100 serial passages (1,200 viral generations) in primary human airway cultures, nearly two-thirds (40 of 63) of the amino-acid mutations that swept to fixation occurred at positions that have been ≥90% invariant across 58 years of global human circulation—leading the authors to conclude that flu proteins have surprisingly weak intrinsic constraints. Running their open dataset (AMC-LAEB/LTEE-flu) through HyphAeon and ChronAeon in under 25 seconds resolves this paradox: those globally conserved positions actually sit under stronger average structural constraint than unmutated positions ($p = 1.9 \times 10^{-13}$), but the specific amino-acid changes that fixed in the lab slipped through narrow, contextually tolerated side doors (median zero-shot Digital DMS rank 2.0 out of 19 possible substitutions, $p = 4.2 \times 10^{-12}$).

🚪 Rank 2.0 / 19

Out of 19 possible amino-acid swaps at each site, the 40 mutations that fixed at globally invariant positions—and even the 8 with negative BLOSUM62 scores—rank at a median of 2.0 / 19 for biophysical tolerance in zero-shot HyphAeon Digital DMS.

⏱️ 1.78× Acceleration
Two-Phase LTEE Clock

ChronAeon AutoClock ($\Delta\mathrm{AICc} = 100.3$) splits the 1,200-generation experiment into an early drift-buffered waiting phase (passages 0–40) and a 1.78× faster selective-sweep phase (passages 50–100), flagging stalled replicate P60-3A ($z = -3.06$).

🎯 0 / 4,378 False
19 Seconds vs. 20,000 ODE Runs

Without simulating 20,000 ODE drift trajectories or fitting a Hidden Markov Model, hyphaeon temporal confirms 82.5% (52/63) of fixed sweeps across all 10 viral proteins with zero false positives on 4,378 neutral codons ($p = 5.7 \times 10^{-90}$).

🔗 4 Multi-Gene Sectors
Inter-Segment Co-Evolution

hyphaeon epistasis maps 10 co-selected residue pairs ($q \le 6.8 \times 10^{-6}$) across segments, recovering the PB1:181 ↔ PA:606 ↔ HA:36 clonal-interference triad and linking PB2:D740N to PA and NA:G448S ($p_{\mathrm{perm}} = 0.0003$).

In nature, human influenza A/H3N2 is famous for relentless antigenic evolution on the head of its hemagglutinin (HA) surface protein, while the internal polymerase and nucleoprotein machinery stays nearly frozen at the amino-acid level over decades. Why do those internal proteins barely budge in global surveillance? A long-standing view is that their 3D structures are so tightly packed that almost any amino-acid change breaks the viral copying machine.

In a tour-de-force preprint from Emma de Rooij, Katie Hulme, Alvin X. Han, Colin A. Russell, and colleagues at Amsterdam UMC (de Rooij et al., 2026), the authors tested that idea by evolving a 2008 human A/H3N2 isolate (A/Netherlands/177/2008) across 100 serial passages (~1,200 viral generations, 300 cumulative days of replication) in 12 independent primary human airway epithelial (HAE) cultures lacking antibodies and host-to-host transmission bottlenecks. When they compared their 63 fixed amino-acid substitutions against 118,000 global H3N2 sequences from GISAID (1968–2025), they found a surprise: 40 of the 63 fixed mutations (63.5%) landed at positions that have been ≥90% invariant across 58 years of human circulation, including 8 fixed substitutions with negative BLOSUM62 scores. From this, the authors argued that seasonal H3N2 proteins are only weakly constrained intrinsically.

Because the authors openly shared their complete processed variant trajectories, reconstructed haplotypes, and GISAID conservation tables on GitHub and Zenodo, I ran all 10 viral proteins (4,477 codons) and the 6-segment concatenation (121 consensus genomes, 3,765 codons) through HyphAeon and ChronAeon. What it all means in plain language: the global conservation of those H3N2 positions is not an illusion, and the proteins are not loosely constrained. Instead, an amino-acid site can have 18 of its 19 possible mutations locked shut by purifying selection while still leaving one or two residue-specific "side doors" open—and coarse 1992 substitution matrices like BLOSUM62 mistake those open side doors for radical changes.

1. Resolving the Paradox: Locked Sites with Narrow Residue-Specific Side Doors

How can a codon stay 99.9% unchanged for half a century in human epidemics yet rapidly fix a mutation in a laboratory dish? When we run zero-shot Digital Deep Mutational Scanning (hyphaeon dms) on the 2008 founder virus—using only the founder's protein sequence context, with zero information from the passage experiment—we get two measurements for every codon: (1) its overall site plasticity ($\Phi$), which averages how well the site tolerates all 19 possible amino-acid swaps, and (2) the specific substitution rank (`1` to `19`) of the exact amino-acid change that actually occurred in the experiment, where Rank `1` is the single least disruptive change at that position and Rank `19` is the most disruptive.

Looking at both numbers together immediately reconciles the global tree with the laboratory dish (Fig. 1A):

  • The mutated positions really are tightly locked overall: Across the 3,818 globally invariant codons (≥90% conserved in GISAID) that never fixed a mutation in the lab, the median site plasticity is $\Phi = 2.088$. Across the 39 globally invariant codons that did fix a mutation in the lab, the median site plasticity is nearly twice as tight: $\Phi = 1.202$ (Mann-Whitney $p = 1.94 \times 10^{-13}$). Most random amino-acid changes at these sites are strongly deleterious, which is why they remain conserved in nature.
  • The mutations that fixed in the lab walked through the #1 or #2 open side door: When we check which of the 19 possible amino-acid substitutions actually fixed across the 12 replicate populations ($N = 63$ fixed events), their median HyphAeon rank is 2.0 out of 19 (mean 3.10; Wilcoxon test against the random expectation of Rank 10: $p = 4.18 \times 10^{-12}$). For the 40 fixed mutations at globally invariant sites, the median rank is also 2.0 out of 19 ($p = 9.09 \times 10^{-12}$, median $|\Delta\mathrm{LRT}| = 0.061$).
  • Why BLOSUM62 called tolerated swaps "radical": What about the 8 fixed substitutions at globally invariant sites that had negative BLOSUM62 scores? Because BLOSUM62 averages substitution frequencies across thousands of unrelated protein families from 1992, it penalizes swaps like isoleucine-to-threonine or glycine-to-glutamate everywhere. In the local structural context of the 2008 H3N2 proteins, however, all 8 of those "non-conservative" substitutions sit in the top 1 to 5 most tolerated choices at their codon—with a median HyphAeon DMS rank of 2.0 out of 19 (Table 1).

2. A Two-Speed Experimental Clock: The 350-Generation Waiting Room

One of the most striking experimental observations in de Rooij et al. (2026) is that almost no mutations fixed during the first 25 to 35 passages (~300 to 400 viral generations); after that waiting period, selective sweeps ignited across all 12 populations. When we run chronaeon date and chronaeon autoclock on the 121 concatenated 6-segment consensus genomes (1.8 seconds total), this transition emerges directly from the sequence distances without any manual windowing (Fig. 1B):

  • Non-linear rate acceleration ($p = 0.0010$): Operating in the low-divergence metric regime ($d_{90} = 0.0018 < 0.05$ substitutions/site, Spearman $\rho = 0.933$), chronaeon date recovers the exact 2008 founder timestamp ($t_{\mathrm{MRCA}} = 2008.44$, leave-one-out jackknife $\mathrm{SE} = 6.9$ days) and an overall rate of $3.88 \times 10^{-6}$ substitutions/site/day (matching the paper's $3.6 \times 10^{-6}$ subs/site/day), while the restricted cubic spline clock confirms a significant upward bend in substitution rate over time ($F = 11.36, p = 0.0010, \Delta\mathrm{AIC} = -9.12$).
  • Unsupervised two-phase partitioning ($\Delta\mathrm{AICc} = 100.3$): chronaeon autoclock automatically partitions the 121 timepoints into two clock regimes ($K^* = 2$, $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = 65.3$ over a single clock): a slower Waiting Phase (Community 0, $N = 58$, covering passages P0–P40 plus late-fixing replicates 3A and 4C at $\mu_0 = 8.74 \times 10^{-4}$ subs/site/yr) and a 1.78× faster Sweep Phase (Community 1, $N = 63$, covering passages P50–P100 at $\mu_1 = 1.55 \times 10^{-3}$ subs/site/yr). Out of 121 samples, sequence triage flags a single outlier—P60-3A ($z = -3.06$)—pinpointing the exact passage where replicate 3A sat with zero fixed mutations right before its delayed sweep at passage 60.

3. Nineteen Seconds vs. 20,000 ODE Trajectories: Sweeps and Multi-Segment Epistasis

To distinguish adaptive sweeps from random genetic drift across passages, the authors built an ordinary differential equation (ODE) model of viral growth in airway droplets, simulated 20,000 neutral drift trajectories, and trained a 2-state Gaussian Hidden Markov Model (HMM). To test for epistasis across genomic segments, they correlated single-passage frequency jumps ($\Delta f$), which can conflate unlinked mutations that happen to sweep during the same passage window.

What happens if we hand the raw sequence and haplotype alignments straight to hyphaeon temporal, hyphaeon meme, and hyphaeon epistasis (Fig. 2)?

  • Zero false positives across 4,378 neutral codons in 19 seconds: Running hyphaeon temporal (`--time-units generations --sweep-mode fixation`) and hyphaeon meme --attribute across all 4,477 codons of all 10 viral proteins takes 19 seconds on an Apple M-series laptop. Without an ODE simulator or phylogenetic tree, hyphaeon temporal confirms 82.5% (52 of 63) of the individual fixed non-synonymous sweeps while calling 0 false positives out of 4,378 neutral or drift codons ($p = 5.70 \times 10^{-90}$, Fig. 2A). At the same time, single-taxon counterfactual attribution in hyphaeon meme isolates the top parallel and episodic drivers across the proteome: NA:G448S ($\widehat{\mathrm{LRT}} = 10.51, p = 0.0023$), PB2:D740N ($\widehat{\mathrm{LRT}} = 9.96, p = 0.0030$, parallel in 2C, 3B, 4A), PA:D606G/N/E ($\widehat{\mathrm{LRT}} = 8.85, p = 0.0053$), M2:D24N ($\widehat{\mathrm{LRT}} = 8.34, p = 0.0068$, parallel in 3C, 4A, 4B), NS1:D139N ($\widehat{\mathrm{LRT}} = 6.71, p = 0.0157$), and the overlapping-reading-frame sweep NEP:E52K / NS1:R204K ($\widehat{\mathrm{LRT}} = 6.64, p = 0.0162$).
  • Four multi-segment epistatic sectors coupling the polymerase to HA and NA: In 1.79 seconds on the concatenated 6-segment alignment, hyphaeon epistasis identifies 10 co-selected codon pairs ($\mathrm{FDR}\ q \le 6.78 \times 10^{-6}$, Fig. 2B) grouped into four spectral sectors ($p_{\mathrm{perm}} = 0.0003$ to $0.0175$). Sector 2 (PB1:I181M/V ↔ PA:D606G/N/E ↔ HA:V36I, $C = 0.843, p_{\mathrm{perm}} = 0.0059$) recovers the exact inter-segment clonal-interference sweep in population 2B discussed by the authors—where multiple competing amino acids at PB1:181 and PA:606 rise and fall in lockstep ($\mathrm{CESI} = 4.87, q < 10^{-300}$)—and shows that it is also coupled to HA:V36I. Even better, Sector 1 (PB2:D740N ↔ PA:R192H ↔ PA:D419N ↔ NA:G448S, $C = 0.856, p_{\mathrm{perm}} = 0.0003$) connects the paper's #1 parallel polymerase mutation (PB2:D740N) directly to the PA–NA co-evolutionary module in replicate 2C, a link that pairwise $\Delta f$ correlation missed because PB2:D740N started rising one passage earlier.

4. Agentic Protocol: How We Performed the Analysis and Reached Each Conclusion (ASD-STE100 Plain Text)

The plain-text protocol below follows ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English) rules so that any researcher or coding agent can audit and reproduce every number in this post from the raw repository files.

AGENTIC PROTOCOL (ASD-STE100 SIMPLIFIED TECHNICAL ENGLISH)

STEP 1: CLONE THE PUBLIC REPOSITORY AND RECONSTRUCT IN-FRAME CODON ALIGNMENTS
1.1. The agent read the preprint PDF (de Rooij et al., 2026; bioRxiv 2026.10.26.761891v1) and cloned https://github.com/AMC-LAEB/LTEE-flu.
1.2. From data/stock_consensus_cds.fasta, results/snv_trajectories.csv, and results/raw_snv_haplotpes.csv, the agent reconstructed in-frame codon alignments (with terminal stop codons removed) for all 10 viral proteins (PB2, PB1, PA, HA, NP, NA, M1, M2, NS1, NEP; 4,477 codons total) across all 12 populations (2A-2C, 3A-3C, 4A-4F) and 10 sequenced passage timepoints (P10-P100, spanning 120 to 1,200 viral generations) plus the founder (stock|0).
1.3. For whole-genome clock and epistasis analyses, the agent concatenated the 6 non-overlapping coding segments (PB2, PB1, PA, HA, NP, NA) into a 121-taxon alignment of 3,765 codons (11,295 nucleotides).

STEP 2: TEST ZERO-SHOT BIOPHYSICAL CONSTRAINT AND MUTATION RANKS (HYPHAEON DMS)
2.1. The agent ran hyphaeon dms on each of the 10 protein alignments relative to the 2008 founder sequence (stock|0).
2.2. For each codon, hyphaeon dms calculated the site-level mutational plasticity (Phi, mean |Delta LRT| across all 19 missense substitutions) and ranked the 19 possible amino-acid substitutions from 1 (smallest |Delta LRT|, most biophysically tolerated) to 19 (largest |Delta LRT|, most disruptive).
2.3. Result A (Site-Level Constraint): Globally conserved sites (>=90% dominant amino acid in 1968-2025 GISAID) that fixed a mutation in the experiment (N = 39 sites) had a significantly lower median plasticity (Phi = 1.202) than globally conserved sites that never mutated (N = 3,818 sites, median Phi = 2.088; Mann-Whitney p = 1.94 x 10^-13).
2.4. Result B (Residue-Specific Rank): Across all 63 fixed non-synonymous mutation events, the observed amino-acid substitution had a median HyphAeon DMS rank of 2.0 / 19 (mean 3.10; Wilcoxon test vs. random rank 10: p = 4.18 x 10^-12). Across the 40 fixed events at globally conserved sites, the median rank was 2.0 / 19 (mean 3.20, p = 9.09 x 10^-12). Across the 8 fixed events at globally conserved sites with BLOSUM62 < 0, the median rank was 2.0 / 19 (mean 2.50, p = 0.0078; all 8 ranked between 1/19 and 5/19).
2.5. Conclusion 1: Globally conserved H3N2 positions are strongly constrained across average substitutions, but fixed laboratory mutations used specific biophysically permissive amino-acid channels that coarse BLOSUM62 scores misclassified as radical.

STEP 3: TEST MOLECULAR CLOCK ACCELERATION AND TWO-PHASE PARTITIONING (CHRONAEON)
3.1. The agent ran chronaeon date and chronaeon autoclock (-k 6) on the 121-taxon Concat6 alignment (completed in 1.8 seconds).
3.2. Result A: Pairwise 90th-percentile divergence was d_90 = 0.0018 (< 0.05 substitutions/site), confirming the outbreak/LTEE metricity regime (Spearman rho = 0.933, R^2 = 0.728). ChronAeon estimated t_MRCA = 2008.44 (120-iteration jackknife SE = 6.9 days) and a mean substitution rate of 1.42 x 10^-3 substitutions/site/year (3.88 x 10^-6 substitutions/site/day). A restricted cubic spline clock detected significant non-linear rate acceleration across passages (F = 11.36, p = 0.0010, Delta AIC = -9.12).
3.3. Result B: ChronAeon AutoClock selected K* = 2 clock communities (Delta AICc = 100.28, Delta BIC = 65.31 over K = 1), separating Community 0 (early waiting phase P0-P40 plus late-fixing replicates 3A and 4C; N = 58, mu_0 = 8.74 x 10^-4 subs/site/yr, R^2 = 0.769) from Community 1 (post-sweep fixation phase P50-P100; N = 63, mu_1 = 1.55 x 10^-3 subs/site/yr, R^2 = 0.534) and flagging outlier P60-3A (studentized residual z = -3.06).
3.4. Conclusion 2: Sequence divergence alone separates the ~350-generation pre-sweep waiting period from the 1.78x faster selective-sweep phase.

STEP 4: TEST TREE-FREE SELECTION AND MULTI-SEGMENT EPISTASIS (HYPHAEON TEMPORAL, MEME, EPISTASIS)
4.1. The agent ran hyphaeon temporal (--time-units generations --sweep-mode fixation) and hyphaeon meme (--attribute) on all 10 proteins, and hyphaeon epistasis on Concat6 (completed in 21 seconds total).
4.2. Result A: Across all 4,477 codons, hyphaeon temporal identified 50 confirmed fixation sweep codons (p_perm <= 0.05): 50 of 99 non-drift codons vs. 0 of 4,378 neutral/drift codons (Fisher's exact p = 5.70 x 10^-90), confirming 52 of 63 individual fixed NS mutation trajectories (82.5%) while rejecting 763 of 787 drift trajectories (97.0%).
4.3. Result B: hyphaeon epistasis identified 10 co-selected codon pairs at FDR q <= 6.78 x 10^-6 organized into 4 multi-segment sectors: Sector 1 (PB2:D740N, PA:R192H, PA:D419N, NA:G448S; C = 0.856, p_perm = 0.0003), Sector 2 (PB1:I181M/V, PA:D606G/N/E, HA:V36I; C = 0.843, p_perm = 0.0059), Sector 3 (PB2:V105M, NA:E435K; C = 0.958, p_perm = 0.0109), and Sector 4 (PB2:S590N, NA:D125N; C = 0.942, p_perm = 0.0175).
4.4. Conclusion 3: Tree-free temporal and manifold-projection operators recover the selective sweeps and multi-segment epistatic networks in seconds without ODE drift simulations or pairwise Delta-f correlation artifacts.
