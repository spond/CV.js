One Spike Mutation, a Chimeric Rodent, and the Ship Doctor's Sub-Clade: Decoding the 2026 MV Hondius Andes Virus Outbreak

In early 2026, Andes virus—the only hantavirus known to spread directly from person to person—triggered a multi-country outbreak of fatal cardiopulmonary syndrome aboard the Antarctic expedition vessel MV Hondius. A new genomic study traced the virus to Southern Chile; re-analyzing the expanded public sequence record with the *aeon toolkit narrows the evolutionary gap by seven years, uncovers a hidden segment-swapping rodent genome, and pinpoints the single surface mutation that defines the shipboard strain.

💡 What Does This All Mean? The MV Hondius virus did not need to reinvent its replication machinery to spread between humans on a cruise ship: 100% of mutations on its polymerase and nucleocapsid segments were silent, leaving a single surface glycoprotein substitution (M:T516I) and a single shipboard transmission marker (L:C2144T) to trace how a trans-Andean rodent virus boarded an expedition vessel and sparked a multi-generation chain.

Yesterday, Ulloa-Zepeda, Parker, Gangavarapu, Rambaut, Andersen, Vial, and an international consortium posted a rapid genomic investigation (medRxiv 2026.11.04.26366130) of the 2026 MV Hondius Andes virus (ANDV) outbreak, in which 13 passengers and crew members across six countries developed hantavirus cardiopulmonary syndrome. By sequencing clinical samples and sharing the data openly on Pathoplexus, the authors showed that all shipboard cases clustered inside a single Southern Chilean sub-clade (B.1.2.2) closest to long-tailed pygmy rice rats (Oligoryzomys longicaudatus) trapped in Toltén in 2013.

Because the preprint froze its public background dataset in May 2026, two questions remained open: why did Bayesian clock models place the divergence between the 2013 Toltén rodents and the 2026 shipboard outbreak all the way back in August 2000, and what changed in the viral proteins during that evolutionary gap? Pulling the full November 2026 Pathoplexus release—which adds 37 newly released Chilean and Argentine genomes—and running the three tools of the *aeon suite (ChronAeon, RhizAeon, and HyphAeon) in under 45 seconds on a laptop resolves both questions.

1. Filling the 13-Year Gap: Trans-Andean Circulation and a Chimeric Rodent

Andes virus carries its genome on three separate RNA segments: a Large (L) segment encoding the viral polymerase, a Medium (M) segment encoding the Gn/Gc surface glycoprotein spikes, and a Small (S) segment encoding the nucleocapsid shell. Between May and October 2026, laboratories in Chile and Argentina deposited two waves of sequences into Pathoplexus: 13 three-segment genomes from Chilean patients sampled between 2012 and 2025, and 24 genomes from Neuquén and Chubut in Argentina sampled through 2025.

Once split database accessions are linked by patient isolate, those releases double the size of Clade B.1.2.2 from 8 to 17 background genomes and fill the 2013–2026 sampling gap. A 2012 human patient from Los Ríos, Chile (Patient 1236, PP_007NVJJ.1) is only 26 coding mutations away from MV Hondius on the M segment—closer than any of the 2013 Toltén rodent genomes (27–29 mutations).

With 2012–2025 samples anchoring the timeline, chronaeon date calibrates the M-segment clock in 0.57 seconds without requiring Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling (Figure 1A). The divergence between the 2013 Toltén rodent lineage and the MV Hondius ancestor shifts forward by 7.3 years, from August 2000 to ~2007.9 (95% CI: 2003.4–2010.8). Discrete phylogeography (chronaeon phylogeo) also finds strong support (Bayes Factor = 89–248) for two-way viral movement across the low mountain passes linking La Araucanía and Los Ríos in Chile with Neuquén in Argentina, showing that B.1.2.2 circulates across a trans-Andean corridor rather than solely within a 50-kilometer radius around two 2013 rodent traps.

ChronAeon also explains why the preprint's S-segment molecular clock disagreed with its L and M estimates. Using Fieller's theorem—which tests whether a regression slope is far enough from zero to yield a finite confidence interval (requiring g < 1)—the 3.4 kb M segment (g = 0.11) and 6.5 kb L segment (g = 0.82) pass, whereas the short 1.3 kb S segment fails outright (Fieller g = 2.25 ≥ 1, 95% CI: [−∞, 1999.6]). On a 428-codon gene evolving under intense purifying constraint, there are too few substitutions within a single sub-lineage to anchor an independent root date.

Even more striking is what happens when rhizaeon tests all 105 complete three-segment genomes for segment swapping (Figure 1B). In the preprint, Lanco rodent R600-UDD (PP_00701HR.1, trapped in 2013) was grouped alongside Toltén rodents R175-UDD and R190-UDD as part of the immediate B.1.2.2 neighborhood of the outbreak. RhizAeon flags R600-UDD as an inter-clade segment chimera (p = 3.24 × 10⁻¹¹): its L segment is nearly identical to Lanco B.1.2.2 rodent R589-UDD (1 SNP apart; 50 SNPs from MV Hondius), but at the L→M boundary its M and S segments jump to the Los Lagos / Puerto Octay B.1.2.1 clade (93 M-SNPs from MV Hondius, versus 26–29 for genuine B.1.2.2 members). By contrast, the MV Hondius outbreak virus is a pure, non-reassortant B.1.2.2 genome across all three segments.

2. Forty-Seven Silent Mutations and a Single Surface Change (M:T516I)

When a rodent virus causes a multi-generation human cluster on a cruise ship, a natural question is whether its replication machinery adapted to human cells. Comparing the coding sequences of MV Hondius against its closest Toltén rodent neighbors with HyphAeon reveals a remarkable evolutionary filter:

  • On the L segment (2,153 codons of the RNA polymerase), MV Hondius differs from Toltén rodent R175-UDD at 36 nucleotide positions. Every single one—36 out of 36 (100%)—is a silent (synonymous) change that leaves the polymerase amino-acid sequence completely unchanged.
  • On the S segment (428 codons of the nucleocapsid protein), MV Hondius differs from Toltén rodent R190-UDD at 11 nucleotide positions. Again, 11 out of 11 (100%) are silent changes, leaving the nucleocapsid protein 100% identical to its 2013 rodent counterpart.
  • On the M segment (1,138 codons of the Gn/Gc surface glycoprotein), 27 of 29 nucleotide differences vs. R175-UDD are silent. Of the two amino-acid changes, M:M288I was already present in 2012 Chilean patient 1236, leaving M:T516I (ACA → ATA) in the Gn surface ectodomain as the only unique amino-acid substitution on the MV Hondius branch across all 3,719 codons of the viral proteome.

Is M:T516I sitting in a neutral variable loop, or in a functionally sensitive region of the spike? Running hyphaeon dms, meme, and phenotype across all 1,138 codons of the M glycoprotein provides three converging lines of evidence (Figure 2A):

  1. Structural rigidity: Codon 516 sits in a tightly constrained pocket of the Gn ectodomain with mutational tolerance Φ₅₁₆ = 0.571—the 11th percentile of tolerance across the entire M glycoprotein (mean Φ = 1.943).
  2. Adjacency to a known human-to-human outbreak loop: Codon 516 lies immediately adjacent to an exposed surface loop (M:510–515) where hyphaeon meme detects episodic positive selection (M:513 p = 0.0052) driven by substitutions during the 2018–2019 Epuyén human-to-human superspreading outbreak in Argentina.
  3. Top phylogenetic-trait coupling: When hyphaeon phenotype scans the M alignment for amino-acid states correlated with the outbreak branch using phylogenetic permutations, M:516 ranks #1 across the entire M segment (Spearman ρ = 0.748, FDR q = 4.76 × 10⁻¹³).

3. Shipboard R0 = 1.40 and the Ship Doctor's Transmission Chain (L:C2144T)

Usually, Andes virus infections represent dead-end spillovers from rodents to humans, with an effective reproduction number at the self-limiting threshold of 1.0. Applying chronaeon dynamics with the empirical 21-day human serial interval separates background rodent-to-human spillover across Lineage B (R₀ = 1.001, 95% CI: 1.000–1.001) and the B.1.2.2 corridor (R₀ = 1.013) from self-sustaining human-to-human spread aboard MV Hondius (R₀ = 1.40, 95% CI: 1.19–1.64, doubling time 42.9 days) and the 2018–2019 Epuyén superspreading outbreak (R₀ = 1.50 overall; 4.86 during the initial burst) (Figure 2B).

Because the preprint masked partial genomes and restricted its within-outbreak comparison to 10 complete sequences, it reported zero nucleotide differences among shipboard patients. Auditing all 22 sequenced MV Hondius genomes available in Pathoplexus through November 2026 uncovers an informative split at nucleotide 2,144 of the L segment (codon P703P) that reconstructs the second wave of shipboard transmission:

  • The Founder Genotype (C2144, n = 7 L-covered cases): All early April-onset passengers and the expedition guide—Cases 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, and 13 (symptom onset April 1–27)—carry the ancestral cytosine (C) shared with all Chilean rodent and human B.1.2.2 genomes.
  • The Ship-Doctor Sub-Clade (T2144, n = 6 L-covered cases): A C→T transition (L:C2144T) first appears in Case 5 (PP_006W3U9.2, the ship's doctor, symptom onset April 30), who treated the initial wave of ill crew members in mid-April. That exact T2144 marker is then inherited by Canadian Case 11 (PP_006XDHK.6 and PP_006XDJH.6, symptom onset May 14) and by all four Dutch passengers sequenced after disembarking in Tenerife, Spain between May 18 and May 22 (PP_007N329.1, PP_007N50B.1, PP_00843KA.1, and PP_00843Q0.1)—identifying the late-April shipboard link that carried the outbreak into mid-May.
⚡ Why Fast, Composable Tools Matter During an Outbreak

Ulloa-Zepeda and colleagues delivered an essential public-health service by sequencing clinical samples across six countries and depositing them openly on Pathoplexus. As additional background and follow-up genomes arrive over the ensuing weeks, pairing open repositories with sub-minute tools (ChronAeon, RhizAeon, and HyphAeon) lets genomic epidemiologists continuously update molecular clocks, screen out chimeric rodent reference strains, rank candidate surface adaptations, and resolve person-to-person transmission chains on a standard laptop.

4. Agentic Protocol (ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English)

The following plain-text protocol follows Specification ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English). It defines the exact commands and verification checks so that an autonomous coding agent or researcher can reproduce every result in this post from the public Pathoplexus records. 

PROTOCOL: MV HONDIUS ANDES VIRUS (*AEON RE-ANALYSIS)
STANDARD: ASD-STE100 (SIMPLIFIED TECHNICAL ENGLISH)
TOTAL RUNTIME: 41.2 SECONDS ON APPLE M-SERIES CPU

STEP 1: PREPARE CURATED ALIGNMENTS AND METADATA
1.1 Download all Andes virus (ANDV) sequences and metadata from Pathoplexus
    (Release 2026-11; 270 sequences across segments L, M, and S).
1.2 Reconcile split isolate labels: link Chilean human L-segment accessions
    ("Patient_<ID>") with matching M- and S-segment accessions ("<ID>").
1.3 Extract in-frame coding sequences (CDS) for L (6,459 bp), M (3,414 bp),
    and S (1,284 bp). Write comma-separated metadata to:
    data/prepared/andv_curated_metadata.csv

STEP 2: CALIBRATE MOLECULAR CLOCKS AND PHYLOGEOGRAPHY (CHRONAEON)
2.1 Run tree-free PGLS clock calibration on Clade B.1.2.2 (M, L, and S):
    chronaeon date -a data/prepared/B122_M.fasta \
      -d data/prepared/B122_M_dates.tsv -o results/chronaeon_date/B122_M.json
2.2 Verify Fieller identifiability statistic (g < 1.0):
    - Segment M: R2 = 0.732, Fieller g = 0.111 (PASS), t_MRCA = 1989.55,
      Tolten-Hondius split = 2007.9 (95% CI: 2003.4 to 2010.8).
    - Segment L: R2 = 0.320, Fieller g = 0.818 (PASS).
    - Segment S: R2 = 0.130, Fieller g = 2.255 >= 1.0 (UNBOUNDED CLOCK).
2.3 Run discrete phylogeography across 14 geographic regions:
    chronaeon phylogeo -a data/prepared/ANDV_M_cds.fasta \
      -l data/prepared/ANDV_M_locs.tsv --bayes-factors \
      -o results/chronaeon_phylogeo/ANDV_M_phylogeo.json
2.4 Verify trans-Andean dispersal Bayes Factors (BF > 80 between
    La Araucania / Los Rios, Chile and Neuquen, Argentina).

STEP 3: DETECT INTER-SEGMENT REASSORTMENT (RHIZAEON)
3.1 Concatenate L + M + S coding alignments for all 105 three-segment isolates
    (11,157 bp; segment boundaries at 6,459 bp and 9,873 bp).
3.2 Run mosaic breakpoint detection:
    rhizaeon -i data/prepared/ANDV_concat_LMS.fasta \
      -o results/rhizaeon/ANDV_concat_LMS
3.3 Verify that Lanco rodent PP_00701HR.1 (R600-UDD) is flagged as an
    inter-clade L (B.1.2.2) / M+S (B.1.2.1) reassortant (p = 3.24e-11),
    and that all MV Hondius outbreak genomes are non-reassortant B.1.2.2.

STEP 4: MAP SELECTION AND MUTATIONAL TOLERANCE (HYPHAEON)
4.1 Run Digital DMS, MEME, and Phenotype association on L, M, and S:
    hyphaeon dms -a data/prepared/ANDV_M_cds.fasta \
      --focal-strain PP_006W3RV.2 -o results/hyphaeon_dms/M_dms_hondius.csv
    hyphaeon meme -a data/prepared/ANDV_M_cds.fasta \
      -o results/hyphaeon_meme/M_meme.csv
    hyphaeon phenotype -a data/prepared/ANDV_M_cds.fasta \
      --phenotype data/prepared/ANDV_M_outbreak_trait.tsv \
      -o results/hyphaeon_phenotype/M_pheno_outbreak.csv
4.2 Verify that 36/36 L-segment SNPs (vs R175-UDD) and 11/11 S-segment SNPs
    (vs R190-UDD) are synonymous, and that M:T516I is the sole unique
    outbreak amino-acid substitution (Phi_516 = 0.571, 11th percentile;
    phenotype rank #1, FDR q = 4.76e-13).

STEP 5: ESTIMATE R0 AND TRACE INTRA-OUTBREAK CHAINS (CHRONAEON DYNAMICS)
5.1 Estimate basic reproduction number R0 with a 21-day serial interval:
    chronaeon dynamics -t data/prepared/trees/HONDIUS_L.nwk \
      -m data/prepared/andv_curated_metadata.csv \
      --serial-interval 21.0 --serial-interval-sd 6.0 --kernel gamma \
      -o results/chronaeon_dynamics/HONDIUS_L_dynamics.json
5.2 Verify R0 = 1.40 (95% CI: 1.19 to 1.64) on MV Hondius versus R0 = 1.001
    across the Lineage B reservoir. Inspect nucleotide 2,144 of the unmasked
    L alignment to confirm C2144 in early April cases (n = 7) and T2144
    arising in Case 5 (Ship Doctor, PP_006W3U9.2) and shared by 5 mid-May
    Canadian and Dutch passenger genomes.
